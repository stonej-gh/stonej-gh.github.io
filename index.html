The lab notebooks

Three machine-learning experiments, written up as they happened, failures included. The newest is the owl: a full-size vision transformer that learned to see from 142 million photos with no labels, written out in plain code and set to naming plants from a grocery store with nothing trained at all. The other two are tiny networks: one teaches itself to fly a spaceship around a black hole with 6,220 numbers in its head, the other watches raw game frames and paints every ship, every laser, and the hole with 28,000. All three ship with the code that produced them.

Every notebook is written at three depths, and you choose per chapter: the plain story anyone can read; ▸ FOR THE LEARNERS, which rebuilds the machinery from first principles with no machine-learning background assumed; and ▸ FOR THE DATA SCIENTISTS, the working notebook in the field's own language.

Notebook 01 · the pilot

Half a worm, flying a spaceship

A network the size of half a worm's brain starts with zero knowledge and teaches itself to fly, fight, cheat, get caught, and bend lasers around the black hole. With the three-minute film.

6,220 params · reinforcement learning · self-play · sim-to-real

Notebook 02 · the spotter

The net that watches the screen

The pilot got its senses served on a plate. Real systems get pixels. A 28,000-parameter network watches raw frames and paints what it sees, then gets squeezed into 8-bit integers you can't tell from the original.

28k params · convolutional segmentation · int8 · pixels in, game out
