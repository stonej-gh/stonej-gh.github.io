The lab notebooks

Three machine-learning experiments, written up as they happened, failures included. The current one is a full-size vision transformer that identifies plants in the western United States, fine-tuned at home on one GPU and running on a phone. The other two are tiny networks: one teaches itself to fly a spaceship around a black hole with 6,220 numbers in its head, the other watches raw game frames and paints every ship, every laser, and the hole with 28,000. Those two are small enough to read end to end, and both ship with the code that produced them.

Every experiment is written at three depths, and you choose per chapter: the plain story anyone can read; ▸ FOR THE LEARNERS, which rebuilds the machinery from first principles with no machine-learning background assumed; and ▸ FOR THE DATA SCIENTISTS, the working notebook in the field's own language.

Notebook 03 · the plant identifier · not written up yet

A plant identifier for the western US

The current work. A DINOv2 vision transformer, fine-tuned at home on one GPU and scoped to 4,191 species a phone meets in eleven western states, running on the iPhone's Neural Engine in under a second. The modern vision stack explained from attention up, a port proven against golden vectors, a measured unsure state, and a field loop where real testers tell the model where it is wrong.

87M params · ViT-B/14 · attention, transformers, self-supervision · field feedback

Half a worm, flying a spaceship

A network the size of half a worm's brain starts with zero knowledge and teaches itself to fly, fight, cheat, get caught, and bend lasers around the black hole. With the three-minute film.

Notebook 02 · the eye

The net that watches the screen

The pilot got its senses served on a plate. Real systems get pixels. A 28,000-parameter network watches raw frames and paints what it sees, then gets squeezed into 8-bit integers you can't tell from the original.

28k params · convolutional segmentation · int8 · pixels in, game out
